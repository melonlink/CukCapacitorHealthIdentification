\title{Topology-Synchronous Decoupled C--ESR Identification\\
for the Energy-Transfer Capacitor in \Cuk\ Converters}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The energy-transfer capacitor in a \Cuk\ converter is both a power-transfer element and a reliability-sensitive component, but ordinary ripple measurements couple its capacitance and equivalent series resistance (ESR). This paper develops physically direction-decoupled, topology-synchronous observations that exploit the inherent current commutation between $+i_{L1}$ and $-i_{L2}$ without diagnostic injection. Timestamped fits in safe pre- and post-edge windows reconstruct an ESR-dominant voltage discontinuity, whereas in-state charge integration yields a capacitance-dominant row after ESR compensation. Positive finite-window information bounds establish local identifiability under valid continuous-conduction-mode conditions. A topology-synchronous direction-specific recursive least-squares estimator (\TSRLS) provides the primary lightweight realization; a structured linear time-varying Kalman estimator (\TSKF) is retained for uncertainty-aware reporting. On 48 blind operating/health cases evaluated under four noise profiles and four residual timing offsets, \TSRLS\ achieved mean capacitance and ESR absolute percentage errors of $0.3727\%$ and $0.2233\%$ and a mean internal convergence time of $13.21$ switching cycles. It also gave the better aggregate tracking tradeoff over $0.1$--$100$~s degradation ramps, while \TSKF\ produced better joint interval coverage. A device-realistic TMS320F28379D simulation yielded worst-case 95th-percentile capacitance and ESR errors of $1.6425\%$ and $2.6109\%$. The results support the observation design and constrained embedded feasibility within simulation; hardware and accelerated-aging validation remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper converted the normal bidirectional energy transfer of a \Cuk{} converter into two topology-synchronous capacitor-health observations---a timestamp-reconstructed switching-edge row for ESR and an ESR-compensated safe-window charge row for capacitance---and proved positive finite-window information bounds that establish local C--ESR identifiability under valid CCM gates. On these observations, the supervised-reset estimator \TSSRKE{} closed the tracking--confidence gap of the unsupervised kernels: it reproduced its Kalman parent exactly in steady state with near-nominal $88.93\%$ simultaneous interval coverage, and settled abrupt capacitance and ESR steps in $1.52$ and $0.48$~ms where the parent froze, at four extra multiplications per row.

On the blind matrix, replacing the generic mixed observation with the proposed set reduced capacitance MAPE from $7.0366\%$ to $0.3727\%$ with the RLS kernel unchanged---the observation design, not estimator complexity, carries the largest gain. \TSSRKE{} then achieved $0.3011\%/0.6446\%$ C/ESR MAPE, while the lightweight \TSRLS{} baseline retained the best static ESR error, ramp tracking, and cost, and remains recommended where only smooth-degradation point tracking is required. Supplementary studies preserved pipeline accuracy under closed-loop regulation, quantified availability down to the light-load boundary, and kept noise-driven error within small multiples of the conditional noise-only CRLB at the commissioned configuration; a constrained F28379D simulation supported device-realistic feasibility with worst p95 errors of $1.6425\%/2.6109\%$.

Hardware and accelerated-aging studies must still validate AFE common-mode behavior, ringing, temperature dependence, calibration transfer, and measured execution time before experimental or lifetime claims are warranted.

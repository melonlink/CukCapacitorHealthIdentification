\begin{abstract}
The energy-transfer capacitor in a \Cuk\ converter is a reliability-critical power-transfer element, but ripple measurements couple its capacitance and equivalent series resistance (ESR). This paper proposes topology-synchronous, direction-decoupled observations that exploit the current commutation between $+i_{L1}$ and $-i_{L2}$ without diagnostic injection: timestamped fits in guarded edge windows reconstruct an ESR-dominant discontinuity, and in-state charge integration yields a capacitance-dominant row. Positive finite-window information bounds establish local identifiability under valid continuous-conduction-mode conditions. On these observations, a supervised-reset Kalman estimator (\TSSRKE) augments the Joseph-form kernel with a predeclared two-time-scale supervisor over accepted and gate-rejected innovations, restoring gain after abrupt health changes without disturbing interval calibration. Evaluation covers 48 blind cases across four noise profiles and four timing skews, with abrupt-step, degradation-ramp, and TMS320F28379D device-realistic scenarios. Simulation results show that the proposed observations alone reduce mean capacitance error from $7.0366\%$ to $0.3727\%$ with the RLS kernel unchanged, and that \TSSRKE\ attains $0.3011\%/0.6446\%$ capacitance/ESR error with $88.93\%$ joint coverage while reproducing its parent exactly in steady state. Abrupt capacitance and ESR steps are settled within $1.52$ and $0.48$~ms, where the unsupervised kernel does not recover; worst 95th-percentile errors of $1.6425\%/2.6109\%$ support device-realistic feasibility under the modeled DSP constraints.
\end{abstract}
